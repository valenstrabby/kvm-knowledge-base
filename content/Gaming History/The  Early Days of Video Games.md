
# The Early Days of Video Games

## Introduction 

Video games began long before modern consoles, computers and smartphones. During the 1940s and 1950s, scientists and engineers experimented with computers and electronic displays. Some of these experiments eventually led to games that allowed people to interact with machines in new ways.

## Early Electronics Games

### Tennis for Two

One important early game was **Tennis for Two**, created by physicist William Higginbotham in 1958. The game was displayed on an oscilloscope and allowed two players to play electronic tennis using separate controllers. It was created as an interactive demonstration for visitors at Brookhaven National Laboratory.

![[tennis for tow.jpeg|355]] 

The game was very different from modern video games because it did not use a television or a computer monitor like the ones we know today. However it showed that electronic technology could be used for interactive entertainment.

### Spacewar!

In the 1960s, students at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology created **Spacewar!** on a PDP-1 computer. The game featured two spaceships that players could control while competing against each other. It became influential among other computer enthusiasts and spread to other universities. 

## The Birth of Home Video Games

### Ralph Baer and the Brown Box

Engineer Ralph Baer began exploring the idea of using television to play games in 1966. By 1967, Baer and his colleagues had created early video games prototypes. Their work eventually resulted in the **Brown Box**, a prototype for a multiplayer video game system. 

![[brown box.jpg|330]]

The Brown Box was licensed to Magnavox and became the **Magnavox Odyssey** in 1972. The Odyssey became the first home video game console that could be played using a television.

![[magnavox odyssey.jpg|384]]
## Why the Early Games were Important

The early development of video games introduced ideas that are still important today: 

- Interactive entertainment
- Multiplayer gaming
- Using television as gaming displays
- Electronic controllers
- Computer-generated games
- Home gaming systems

> The early history of video games shows how experiments with computers and televisions eventually became a major form of entertainment.
## Connecting Gaming History

The early development of video games led to the growth of arcades and home consoles. This connects to the [[The Golden Age of Arcade Games]], which explains how arcade gaming became popular. 

The development of home systems also connects to [[The Rise of Home Console]], which explains how consoles brought video games into people's homes.

The history of gaming also helps explain [[The Evolution of Online Gaming]] and how video games eventually developed into connected experiences. 

## Conclusion 

The early days of video games were a period of experimentation and innovation. Games such as Tennis for Two and Spacewar! demonstrated that computers and electronic displays could be used for interactive entertainment. Later, Ralph Baer's Brown Box and the Magnavox Odyssey helped bring videogames into people's homes. These early developments helped shape the foundation for the gaming industry that followed.
