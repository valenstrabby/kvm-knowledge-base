
# The Rise of Home Console

## Introduction

Home video games changed the way people experienced video games. Instead of going to an arcade, players could now connect a console to a TV and play games at home. Early systems helped establish gamins as a form of entertainment that can be enjoyed by families and friends.

## The Atari 2600

### Bringing Games Home
 ![[Atari.png|354]]

In September 1977, Atari released the Atari 2600, originally called the Atari Video Computer System (Atari VCS). It became an important home console because it used interchangeable game cartridges. Players could purchase different games instead of being limited to games built into the console. 

### Popular Games

The Atari 2600 helped bring arcade-style games into homes of many. Some notable games associated with the system included: 

- Space Invaders
- Pac-Man
- Asteroids
- Adventure

The popularity of cartridge-based consoles encouraged more companies to develop games for home systems. 

## The Video Game Crash

### Problems in the Video Game Market

The rapid growth of the home console market eventually created problems. During the early 1980s, there were many consoles and a large number of games available. The Smithsonian notes than an oversupply of games, including many lower-quality tittles, contributed to the industry crash in  1983-1984. 

> The history of home consoles show how quickly new technology can change an entire entertainment industry. 

## Nintendo's Impact

### The NES 
![[NES.png|353]]

In October 1985, Nintendo released the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES) in North America. The system helped revive the American home console market after the crash and helped establish video games as a lasting part of mainstream entertainment. 
## Conclusion

The rise of home consoles was an important part of video games history. Systems such as the Atari 2600 made it possible for players to build collections of games and enjoy them at home. Later, consoles such as the NES helped the industry recover and continue to develop. The history of home consoles also connects to [[The Golden Age of Arcade Games]] because many home systems brought arcade-style games into people's living rooms.
